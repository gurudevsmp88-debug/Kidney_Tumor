\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Setting.}
We reconstruct a reference equirectangular panorama $I_1$ of width $W$, given a monocular depth prior
and optionally one or more partner panoramas $I_k$ with relative poses $(\mathbf{R}_k,\bvec_k)$
expressed in the reference camera frame (from GNSS/IMU or structure from motion
\cite{schoenberger2016sfm}). Every pixel defines a unit ray $\dvec$; we estimate its metric depth
$z$ (distance along $\dvec$) and an uncertainty. All fusion happens in log-depth $\ell=\log z$, where
relative errors are additive.

\subsection{Closed-form spherical triangulation uncertainty}
\label{sec:sigma}

Consider one partner at $\bvec$ and the point $\mathbf{X}=z\dvec$. In the triangle
$(\mathbf{0},\bvec,\mathbf{X})$ let $\alpha$ be the angle at the reference camera between $\dvec$ and
$\bvec$, $\beta$ the angle at the partner, and $\gamma=\pi-\alpha-\beta$ the parallax angle at
$\mathbf{X}$. The law of sines gives
\begin{equation}
  z = \|\bvec\|\,\frac{\sin\beta}{\sin\gamma}.
\end{equation}
Matching perturbs the two observed directions by small angles within the epipolar plane;
out-of-plane components do not change $z$ to first order. Differentiating,
$\partial z/\partial\alpha=\|\bvec\|\sin\beta\cos\gamma/\sin^2\gamma$ and
$\partial z/\partial\beta=\|\bvec\|\sin\alpha/\sin^2\gamma$.
A rotation error of the partner with per-axis standard deviation $\sigma_R$ turns its ray within the
epipolar plane by the same angle. A position error with per-axis standard deviation $\sigma_t$ shifts
the partner ray; only its in-plane component perpendicular to that ray matters, and moves the
intersection by $\delta/\sin\gamma$. With angular matching precision $\sigma_\theta$ on both rays we
obtain
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_z^2 = \frac{\|\bvec\|^2}{\sin^4\gamma}\Big[\sigma_\theta^2\sin^2\beta\cos^2\gamma
  + (\sigma_\theta^2+\sigma_R^2)\sin^2\alpha\Big] + \frac{\sigma_t^2}{\sin^2\gamma}.
  \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
In the far field ($z\gg\|\bvec\|$, $\beta\to\pi-\alpha$) and without pose error this reduces to
$\sigma_z\approx\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_\theta z^2/(\|\bvec\|\sin\alpha)$: quadratic in depth, inversely
proportional to the baseline, and divergent at the epipoles $\alpha\in\{0,\pi\}$. We compute all sines
from cross-product norms; the naive $\sqrt{1-\cos^2}$ loses all precision in single precision
for the tiny parallax angles of distant points. A Monte Carlo simulation with isotropic angular
noise on both rays and random pose perturbations agrees with \cref{eq:sigma} within 0.5\% across
all tested configurations (unit tests in the code release).

We denote by $\U(\dvec)=\sigma_z/z$ the log-depth uncertainty for \emph{unit} matching precision
$\sigma_\theta = 2\pi/W$ (one pixel) without pose error, and by $P(\dvec)$ the pose-only term. $\U$
contains all of the geometry: depth, baseline length and direction, and panorama resolution.
Because $\U$ depends on the unknown depth, we evaluate it at the (scale-aligned) prior depth rather than
at the stereo estimate, which is unreliable exactly where $\U$ is large.

\subsection{Spherical plane-sweep stereo}
\label{sec:stereo}

For each reference ray and log-depth hypothesis we project $z\dvec$ into the partner panorama and
sample it with longitude wrap-around. We sweep 64 log-spaced hypotheses in $[1,200]$\,m, followed by
two refinement passes with 16 hypotheses each around the arg-max, and use the soft-arg-min of the
final pass. The matching cost is either the box-aggregated L1 difference or $1-$ZNCC over a $5\times5$
window; ZNCC is invariant to the gain and bias changes between captures. As a match confidence we use
the distinctiveness of the coarse cost curve, $c=(\operatorname{med}_h C_h-\min_h C_h)/\operatorname{med}_h C_h$,
which vanishes both near the epipoles, where all hypotheses project to almost the same pixel, and in
textureless or occluded regions.

\subsection{Geometry-conditioned calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

\Cref{eq:sigma} is exact \emph{given} $\sigma_\theta$, but the effective matching precision is not a
constant number of pixels. Window aggregation, texture, the cost function and the bounded depth search
all matter. On our benchmark, the implied precision varies from $0.1$\,px near the epipoles to
$0.6$\,px orthogonal to the baseline. We therefore keep the geometry in closed form and calibrate a
monotone map from $\U$ to the observed error:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_s(\dvec) = \sqrt{\big(a(c)\,\U(\dvec)^{\kappa}\big)^2 + P(\dvec)^2},
  \label{eq:calibration}
\end{equation}
with a shared exponent $\kappa$ and a scale $a(c)$ that is piecewise constant and non-increasing in
the match confidence $c$ (six quantile bins). The pose term $P$ has no free parameters. The
parameters are fitted on calibration rays with known depth---in practice the city teacher of
\cref{sec:teacher}. We select $\kappa$ by maximizing a Student-$t$ likelihood ($\nu=3$), which
tolerates occlusions and mismatches. We then fit each $a$ by Gaussian maximum likelihood on the
$t$-inliers, because fusion needs a standard deviation and the $t$-scale under-estimates it by about
20\% for Gaussian errors. Rays deep inside the epipolar cones are excluded from fitting: there the
bounded depth range truncates the errors. $\kappa=1$ with a single bin recovers the pure closed form
with constant $\sigma_\theta$.

\subsection{Epipole-aware fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}

\paragraph{Scale alignment.}
Monocular metric depth is often off by a per-image scale factor. We estimate a global log-scale
offset as the precision-weighted median of $\mu_s-\mu_p$ over well-conditioned rays
($\sigma_s<0.05$, $c>0.5$), with weights $c/\sigma_s^2$, and add it to the prior.

\paragraph{Gated Bayesian fusion.}
Let the prior be $\mathcal{N}(\mu_p,\sigma_p^2)$ in log-depth and partner $k$ give
$\mathcal{N}(\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)$ with confidence $c_k$. Occlusions, moving objects and mismatches
produce gross errors, so we model the disagreement $\delta_k=\mu_k-\mu_p$ as a mixture of an inlier
Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_p^2+\sigma_k^2)$ with prior probability $\pi$ and a uniform outlier
over the log-depth range $L$:
\begin{equation}
  w_k = c_k\,\frac{\pi\,\mathcal{N}(\delta_k;0,\sigma_p^2+\sigma_k^2)}
  {\pi\,\mathcal{N}(\delta_k;0,\sigma_p^2+\sigma_k^2)+(1-\pi)/L}.
\end{equation}
The posterior is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{\sigma^2}=\frac{1}{\sigma_p^2}+\sum_k\frac{w_k}{\sigma_k^2},\qquad
  \mu=\sigma^2\Big(\frac{\mu_p}{\sigma_p^2}+\sum_k\frac{w_k\,\mu_k}{\sigma_k^2}\Big).
\end{equation}
Near the epipoles $\sigma_k\to\infty$ and the result is the scale-aligned prior; orthogonal to the
baseline the stereo dominates. With a single panorama the posterior is the prior. Every operation is
differentiable, so the same module can be placed inside a learned network (\cref{sec:teacher}).

\subsection{City as teacher}
\label{sec:teacher}

A dense capture of one city, with many panoramas per street, is reconstructed once with multi-view
geometry (SfM/MVS \cite{schoenberger2016sfm,schoenberger2016mvs} or surface splatting
\cite{huang2024twodgs}). A teacher point $\mathbf{X}$ observed from centres $\mathbf{C}_i$ at
distances $r_i$ along unit directions $\mathbf{u}_i$ has triangulation information
$\boldsymbol{\Lambda}=\sum_i(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{u}_i\mathbf{u}_i^\top)/(r_i\sigma_\theta)^2$, and
covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1}$, computed in double precision because
single-view points are nearly singular. For a student panorama we render two-layer targets: the first
teacher surface along each ray (visible), and the first surface behind it (amodal: behind parked
cars, around corners), which only other panoramas of the city observe. Each target carries the
variance $\dvec^\top\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\dvec$ along the student ray. A student predicting
$(\mu,\sigma)$ in log-depth is trained with the Gaussian negative log-likelihood whose variance is
$\sigma^2+\dvec^\top\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\dvec/z^2$, so poorly triangulated teacher points supervise
weakly instead of wrongly. The same teacher provides the calibration rays for \cref{eq:calibration}.
Our reference student shares an equirectangular encoder (with longitude wrap-around padding) between
a prior head, the stereo cost volume on learned features, the closed-form uncertainty with a learned
global scale, the fusion of \cref{sec:fusion}, and an amodal second-layer head.
