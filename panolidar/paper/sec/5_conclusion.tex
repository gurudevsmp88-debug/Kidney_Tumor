\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{No real data yet.}
All results in this draft are on procedural scenes with a simulated prior. Real panoramas add
non-Lambertian surfaces, vegetation, sky, rolling-shutter and stitching artefacts, and a prior whose
errors are spatially structured in ways our simulation does not capture. The decisive experiment is
KITTI-360~\cite{liao2022kitti360}: its two 180$^\circ$ fisheye cameras (MEI model~\cite{mei2007single})
cover nearly the full sphere and can be resampled to equirectangular images, and accumulated LiDAR
provides metric ground truth including occluded regions. The protocol is: pairs of frames along
the trajectory at 2--8\,m spacing, priors from Depth Any Panoramas~\cite{lin2026dap} and
DA360~\cite{da360}, calibration on training sequences, and evaluation on held-out sequences
against Splatter-360~\cite{chen2025splatter360}, PanSplat~\cite{zhang2024pansplat} and pointmap
regressors \cite{leroy2024mast3r,wang2025vggt} run on cube faces.

\paragraph{The learned student is not evaluated.}
The city-as-teacher training (\cref{sec:teacher}) is implemented. A small reference student trained
for 400 CPU steps did not converge (median errors far above the prior), so we report no student
results. The teacher is evaluated only as a source of calibration data (\cref{sec:teacher_cal}).
Whether amodal supervision and covariance-weighted teacher losses improve a foundation-model student
requires GPU training and remains open.

\paragraph{Modelling assumptions.}
Partners share the reference image, so treating several partners as independent overstates the
precision. Moving objects are rejected as outliers, and their depth falls back to the prior.
Relative-pose error enters $\sigma_s$ only through a per-axis standard deviation. When poses come from
SfM, their actual covariance should be propagated instead.

\section{Conclusion}
Two panoramas carry precise metric information about a scene, but only away from their epipoles.
We showed that a closed-form, pose-aware triangulation uncertainty, combined with a small
geometry-conditioned calibration of the matching precision, makes this observability explicit.
Fusing on that basis with a monocular prior yields metric depth with calibrated per-ray
uncertainty that falls back gracefully where geometry is uninformative. In controlled experiments it
reduces the error of a simulated foundation prior by more than $4\times$. In-distribution it is
matched by a well-calibrated geometry-blind confidence. It remains calibrated and becomes clearly more
accurate once the relative pose is uncertain, the normal case for street-level capture, and when an
exposure-robust cost is used, where the confidence proxy loses its implicit knowledge of the epipoles. Establishing how close this brings
real street-level panoramas to LiDAR accuracy is the next step.
