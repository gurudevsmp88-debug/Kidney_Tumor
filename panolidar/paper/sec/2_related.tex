\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Monocular panoramic depth.}
The first 360$^\circ$ depth networks combined equirectangular and cubemap
features~\cite{wang2020bifuse,jiang2021unifuse,li2022omnifusion} or merged predictions made on
perspective tangent images~\cite{rey2022360monodepth}. More recent foundation models predict metric
depth for indoor and outdoor panoramas~\cite{lin2026dap,da360}, or lift perspective models such as
Depth Anything~\cite{yang2024depthv2} onto the sphere~\cite{pager2026}. Perspective metric-depth
models~\cite{yin2023metric3d,piccinelli2024unidepth,bochkovskii2025depthpro} have the same basic
limitation: with one image, metric scale comes only from learned priors. We use any of these models as
a prior with its own uncertainty, and correct its scale with geometry.

\paragraph{Multi-view panoramic reconstruction.}
Omnidirectional stereo with spherical sweeps goes back at least to OmniMVS~\cite{won2019omnimvs}.
Generalizable radiance-field and Gaussian-splatting methods for wide-baseline
panoramas~\cite{chen2023panogrf,chen2025splatter360,zhang2024pansplat} also build spherical cost
volumes, but are trained for view synthesis. Pointmap regressors~\cite{wang2024dust3r,leroy2024mast3r,wang2025vggt}
reconstruct from unposed perspective images. As far as we know, none of them models how observable
depth is in each direction, and none outputs a calibrated metric uncertainty.

\paragraph{Triangulation uncertainty.}
First-order error propagation for triangulation is textbook
material~\cite{hartley2003multiple,forstner2016photogrammetric}, and multi-view stereo
pipelines~\cite{schoenberger2016mvs} use the triangulation angle as a heuristic when selecting views.
We write the propagation out for spherical cameras, add relative-pose error, and use the result as the
geometric backbone of a calibrated uncertainty that drives the fusion, instead of a threshold.

\paragraph{Uncertainty in depth estimation.}
Work on learned aleatoric uncertainty~\cite{kendall2017uncertainties} and on uncertainty for
self-supervised depth~\cite{poggi2020uncertainty} is usually judged by how well the uncertainty ranks
errors~\cite{ilg2018uncertainty}. Calibrated regression~\cite{kuleshov2018calibrated} fixes the
scale afterwards. Our calibration is conditioned on a closed-form geometric quantity. That quantity
also carries the relative-pose error, which a learned confidence has no way to observe
(\cref{sec:pose}).

\paragraph{Supervision from additional views.}
Behind the Scenes~\cite{wimbauer2023bts} and its distilled successor~\cite{han2024kdbts} train
single-view density fields on KITTI-360 with extra views as supervision. Our teacher is instead a
metric multi-view reconstruction of an entire city. The covariance of each teacher point weights the
loss, and the same reconstruction supplies amodal targets and calibration data.
